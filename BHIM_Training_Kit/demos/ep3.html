Why this matters

AI cost is a design decision, not a surprise

For managers: ask for cost per 1,000 chats (or per task), not just the monthly bill. Demand a budget cap and an alert before launch, the same way you would for cloud spend.
For engineers: you pay for every token in and out, on every call. Long prompts, long chat history, many retrieved documents and agent loops multiply it. Route to the cheapest model that passes your tests.

Token = a chunk of text, roughly a part of a word. Providers bill per token, usually quoted per million tokens, with input and output priced separately (output is typically more expensive). Many providers offer prompt caching at a discount for repeated input. Check your own provider's current pricing page: prices change often.

Real world check (no incident quoted on purpose)

Where real AI bills grow (patterns, not a specific company)

Biggest model for everythingTeams prototype on the top model and never change it. Simple FAQs then cost the same as hard reasoning.Fix: routing plus an eval to prove the small model is good enough.
Agent loopsAn agent that cannot finish keeps calling tools and the model again. Each loop re-sends the growing context.Fix: step cap, timeout, and hand-off to a human.
Context creepFull chat history plus 10 documents on every turn. Most of the bill is input tokens nobody needed.Fix: trim, summarise, retrieve fewer and better chunks.
No cap, no ownerNobody watches the dashboard until the invoice arrives.Fix: budget cap, 80% alert, a named cost owner.
