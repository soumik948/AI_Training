Episode 4 · Architecture choices

AI or automation? Pick the cheapest tool that works

AutoDesh Motors (fictional) now wants "AI everywhere". BHIM sorts 8 everyday jobs into 4 buckets: a simple rule, an API call, an LLM, or an agent. Too small a tool fails. Too big a tool burns money and adds risk.

🎯 Goal: right-size every AI idea🪜 Tool ladder: rules → API → LLM → agent⏱ 8 to 10 min in a 1-hour session

Why this matters

Four questions before anyone says "let's use AI"

1 · Fixed logic, structured input?Use a rule or a scheduler. Cheap, exact, easy to audit.
2 · Answer sits in another system?Call its API. Live data, no guessing.
3 · Messy language in or out?Use an LLM, grounded in your documents (Ep 2), with a human for anything sent outside.
4 · Many steps, tools, a path that changes?Use an agent, with a step cap (Ep 3), least privilege (Ep 5) and human approval for actions.

For managers: "AI everywhere" is not a strategy. Ask which rung of the ladder each job needs. For engineers: hybrids are normal. Let the LLM understand the question, and let a rule or an API supply the number.

Real incidents (widely reported; open the original news before quoting details)

When the model was asked to own what a rule should own

Dec 2023 · US car dealer chatbotA Chevrolet dealer's ChatGPT-based website bot was talked into "agreeing" to sell an SUV for $1. (Full story in Ep 1.)Lesson: prices come from a rules engine or price API, never from model text.
Feb 2024 · Air Canada chatbotA Canadian tribunal held the airline responsible for a refund policy its chatbot made up. (Full story in Ep 2.)Lesson: fixed policy answers should be looked up and quoted, not generated.
For your own roomAsk the audience for one job in their team where AI was proposed but a rule or a report would do. Use their example, not an invented one.Lesson: the cheapest tool that works usually wins.
